On March Monday, scientists at a university laboratory in Paris reported a study on cancer patients undergoing treatment with cisplatin, where altered DNA repair pathways involving p53 and BRCA1 were observed. The research, conducted in collaboration with the WHO and supported by data from a local hospital clinic, also examined the roles of glucose, insulin, and calcium in cellular metabolism. According to the NIH, these findings may improve therapeutic strategies for tumor management and reduce infection risks in clinical settings.
